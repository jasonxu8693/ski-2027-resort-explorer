# Phone experience strategy · 14 October 2026

The design must turn a first visit into a confident return visit. Fitting everything on a narrow screen is not the acceptance criterion.

## First visit

Within the opening screen, a newcomer should understand the destination, dates and booked base, then recognise how to find travel arrangements, their own preparation and things to do. The trip overview explains the actual trip before asking them to act on an overdue group job. No onboarding carousel, forced tour or assumed knowledge of the old chapter sequence.

## Return visit

Three permanent destinations have stable meanings:

| Destination | Question it answers | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Trip plan | What is our plan, and what do I need to sort? | Overview; travel/base shortcuts; week, bookings, tasks, packing, food, people, costs and reference |
| Places | Where could I go? | Searchable venue directory, visible categories, area filter and Saved collection |
| Guide | What should I know about this trip? | Seven topic-based explanations; no need to read in sequence |

The selected destination, page title and a named parent remain visible on ordinary reading screens. The bottom bar never disappears for a guide, place or organiser section. Photos alone use a dismissible full-screen overlay.

## Moving around

- Bottom destinations provide an escape from every ordinary page. Returning to a destination resumes the last screen; tapping its already-selected tab returns to its overview. Each child also has a plainly labelled parent control.
- Search, filters, Saved selection and list position survive opening a place and coming back. A place reached from a guide names that guide in its return control.
- Guide chapters remember their own reading position. Bookings remember the selected person locally. Existing packing and favourite persistence remain separate from shared group records.
- Direct links use the content's canonical destination and a sensible parent. Contextual links retain the current tab and explicitly name the originating page in Back; only choosing a bottom destination switches tabs.
- A button labelled Photos opens photos in one tap. Directions explicitly opens Maps. A place-detail link is labelled as a place, not as a photo gallery.

## Experience acceptance

Review the journey, not just the final screen: first arrival → understand the trip → find bookings; browse Food → open a place → photos → return to the same filtered list; read lesson guidance → inspect a related venue → return to the same paragraph; move between packing and places without closing a modal or hunting another menu.

Record actual behaviour and tap counts after implementation. Test keyboard focus, deep links, browser Back, short and long content, narrow phones, and desktop. Layout checks do not substitute for watching a first-time person use the result on a real phone.
